# Financial Stability Board (FSB) & IOSCO Guidance on AI in Financial Markets
**Source Identifier**: `FSB_IOSCO_AI_MARKET_CONDUCT`  
**Publishing Body**: Financial Stability Board (FSB) & International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO)  
**Jurisdiction**: International / Global Financial Markets  
**Version**: 2024.1  
**Category**: Finance Regulatory  

---

## Section 1: Systemic Risk & Macroprudential AI Oversight

### Clause 1.1: Mitigation of Algorithmic Herding and Market Procyclicality
Financial regulators and global systemically important institutions (G-SIFIs) must monitor the risk of "algorithmic herding"—where homogeneous AI models and shared foundation models respond identically to market shocks, triggering severe liquidity evaporation, correlated sell-offs, and systemic market flash crashes.

### Clause 1.2: Cross-Market Contagion and Interconnectivity Stress Testing
Institutions deploying autonomous AI trading and risk management models across multiple asset classes must execute quarterly reverse stress testing simulating severe liquidity crises, high-frequency network outages, and cross-market price dislocations.

---

## Section 2: Third-Party Concentration and Cloud Dependency

### Clause 2.1: Monitoring Concentration in AI Foundation Model Providers
Financial institutions must track and report concentration risks arising from reliance on a small number of dominant hyperscale AI model providers and cloud compute vendors. Risk limits must be established to prevent single-point-of-failure vulnerabilities across the financial ecosystem.

### Clause 2.2: Algorithmic Collusion and Tacit Coordination Prevention
Market participants must audit multi-agent reinforcement learning models to ensure pricing algorithms do not engage in tacit algorithmic collusion, price fixing, or anti-competitive market division in securities, foreign exchange, or lending markets.
