# UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence
**Source Identifier**: `UNESCO_AI_ETHICS`  
**Publishing Body**: United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO)  
**Version**: 2021  
**Category**: Core AI Governance  

---

## Article 1: Scope and Aims
This Recommendation addresses the ethical implications of AI systems throughout their lifecycle, from design and development to deployment and decommissioning. It provides a universal normative framework for AI governance.

---

## Article 3: Values

### Clause 3.1: Respect for Human Dignity and Human Rights
AI systems must not be used in ways that undermine human dignity, human rights, or fundamental freedoms. Financial AI systems that affect individual access to credit, insurance, or banking services must preserve the autonomy and dignity of data subjects.

### Clause 3.2: Living in Peaceful, Just, and Interconnected Societies
AI technologies should be leveraged to foster inclusive financial ecosystems that reduce inequity and promote access to economic opportunities for underserved populations.

---

## Article 4: Principles

### Clause 4.1: Proportionality and Do No Harm
AI methods selected must be appropriate and proportional to achieve legitimate aims or objectives. They must not cause disproportionate harm to individuals or communities, particularly in high-stakes financial decisioning.

### Clause 4.2: Fairness and Non-Discrimination
AI actors must promote social justice, fairness, and non-discrimination while taking an active approach to prevent and mitigate discrimination related to AI systems.

### Clause 4.3: Sustainability
AI actors are encouraged to favor AI methods and data practices that limit environmental impact, assess the lifecycle carbon footprint of AI infrastructure, and optimize energy consumption of model training and inference.

---

## Article 5: Policy Areas

### Clause 5.1: Data Governance
Member States should encourage the development of governance frameworks for data used in AI systems, ensuring data quality, integrity, provenance, and compliance with privacy regulations.

### Clause 5.2: Education and Research
Organizations deploying AI systems should invest in workforce AI literacy, training employees on responsible AI practices, governance frameworks, and ethical considerations relevant to their operational domain.

### Clause 5.3: Ethical Impact Assessment
Before deploying AI systems with potential societal impact, organizations should conduct Ethical Impact Assessments to identify, evaluate, and mitigate ethical risks, including those related to bias, privacy, and accountability.
