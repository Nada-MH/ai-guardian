# SAMA Anti-Money Laundering & Counter-Terrorist Financing (AML/CFT) Rules
**Source Identifier**: `SAMA_AML_CFT_RULES`  
**Publishing Body**: Saudi Central Bank (SAMA)  
**Jurisdiction**: Saudi Arabia (KSA)  
**Version**: 2023.2 (Implementing Law on AML Royal Decree M/20)  
**Category**: Saudi GRC & Finance Regulatory  

---

## Section 1: Customer Due Diligence (CDD) & Ultimate Beneficial Ownership (UBO)

### Clause 1.1: Mandatory Verification of Ultimate Beneficial Owners (UBO)
Financial entities must identify and verify the identity of the Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) holding directly or indirectly 25% or more of the capital or voting rights of any corporate customer prior to establishing a business relationship. Relying on self-declarations without independent commercial registry and national ID verification is strictly prohibited.

### Clause 1.2: Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) for Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)
Institutions must implement automated, continuous screening against national and international Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) databases, high-risk jurisdiction sanction lists (UN, FATF, KSA National Counter-Terrorism Committee), and adverse media feeds. Establishing business relationships with PEPs requires prior written approval from the Chief Executive Officer (CEO).

---

## Section 2: Suspicious Transaction Reporting & Automated Screening

### Clause 2.1: Automated Real-Time Sanctions Screening
All domestic and cross-border payment messages (SWIFT, sarie, remittance APIs) must be screened in real time against updated sanction lists prior to execution. Systems must automatically freeze funds and flag transactions matching sanctioned entities without delay.

### Clause 2.2: Mandatory Suspicious Transaction Reports (STR) Submission
When an institution suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are the proceeds of criminal activity or related to terrorist financing, it must submit an official Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) to the Saudi Financial Investigation Unit (SAFIU) within a maximum of forty-eight (48) hours, maintaining strict statutory confidentiality (anti-tipping-off).
