# IEEE 7000-2021 — Standard Model for Addressing Ethical Concerns During System Design
**Source Identifier**: `IEEE_7000_ETHICAL_AI`  
**Publishing Body**: Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE)  
**Jurisdiction**: International / Engineering Standards  
**Version**: 7000-2021  
**Category**: Core AI Governance / Ethics  

---

## Section 1: Value-Based Engineering & Stakeholder Values

### Clause 1.1: Core Human Value Elicitation & Prioritization
Engineering teams designing autonomous and AI systems must systematically identify, analyze, and document the human values (such as dignity, privacy, justice, transparency, and autonomy) of all directly and indirectly affected stakeholders before defining technical specifications.

### Clause 1.2: Ethical Risk Assessment & Harm Modeling
Systems must undergo structured Ethical Risk Modeling to pinpoint scenarios where technical optimization goals (such as accuracy, speed, or revenue maximization) conflict with fundamental ethical duties or generate disproportionate harm to vulnerable demographics.

---

## Section 2: Traceability and Ethical System Requirements

### Clause 2.1: Ethical Value Requirement (EVR) Traceability
Every identified stakeholder ethical value must be translated into verifiable Ethical Value Requirements (EVRs) and mapped directly to architectural components, code modules, and test criteria throughout the engineering lifecycle.

### Clause 2.2: Autonomous Agent Boundaries and Human Agency Preservation
AI systems must be designed to preserve human agency and decision-making authority, preventing coercive algorithmic nudging or unauthorized autonomous actions in high-stakes human domains.
