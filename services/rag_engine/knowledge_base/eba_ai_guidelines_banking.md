# European Banking Authority (EBA) Guidelines on AI in Banking & Credit Risk
**Source Identifier**: `EBA_AI_GUIDELINES_BANKING`  
**Publishing Body**: European Banking Authority (EBA)  
**Jurisdiction**: European Union / International Banking  
**Version**: EBA/GL/2023/08  
**Category**: Finance Regulatory  

---

## Section 1: AI in Internal Ratings-Based (IRB) Credit Risk Models

### Clause 1.1: Prudential Validation of Machine Learning for Credit Scoring
Credit institutions deploying advanced machine learning models (e.g. Gradient Boosting, Deep Neural Networks) for Probability of Default (PD), Loss Given Default (LGD), or Exposure at Default (EAD) estimation under the IRB approach must guarantee full mathematical stability, monotonicity of risk parameters, and conceptual soundness compliant with Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR).

### Clause 1.2: Overfitting Prevention and Out-of-Time Generalization
Credit scoring algorithms must be validated using rigorous out-of-time and out-of-sample datasets spanning full economic credit cycles (including severe macroeconomic downturns) to prevent model overfitting and unpredicted default misclassification.

## Section 2: Consumer Protection & Anti-Discrimination in Retail Lending

### Clause 2.1: Justification of Alternative and Non-Financial Data
Credit institutions are prohibited from using opaque or intrusive alternative data sources (such as social media interactions, device battery levels, or unverified behavioral telemetry) to score consumer creditworthiness. Only relevant, legally permissible, and verified financial data can be incorporated into credit scoring algorithms.

### Clause 2.2: Explainability of Automated Credit Underwriting Decisions
In compliance with consumer credit directives, banks must provide individual credit applicants with specific, granular, and comprehensible reasons whenever an automated loan application is rejected or subjected to higher interest margins.
