# EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation EU 2024/1689)

**Source Identifier**: `EU_AI_ACT_2024`  
**Publishing Body**: European Parliament and Council of the European Union  
**Version**: 2024.1689  
**Category**: Global AI Safety & Governance  

---

### Article 9 — Risk Management System
High-risk AI systems must establish, implement, document, and maintain a continuous, iterative risk management system throughout their entire lifecycle. The system shall estimate and evaluate known and foreseeable risks to health, safety, and fundamental rights, and adopt targeted risk management measures.

### Article 10 — Data and Data Governance
High-risk AI systems which make use of techniques involving the training of models with data shall be developed on the basis of training, validation, and testing data sets that meet quality criteria. Data sets shall be relevant, sufficiently representative, and to the best extent possible free of errors and complete.

### Article 13 — Transparency and Provision of Information to Users
High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way as to ensure that their operation is sufficiently transparent to enable deployers to interpret the system's output and use it appropriately. Systems must be accompanied by instructions for use including technical capabilities and performance boundaries.

### Article 14 — Human Oversight
High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way, including with appropriate human-machine interface tools, that they can be effectively overseen by natural persons during the period in which the AI system is in use. Human overseers must be able to interrupt or stop the system via a kill-switch mechanism.

### Article 15 — Accuracy, Robustness and Cybersecurity
High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way that they achieve an appropriate level of accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity throughout their lifecycle. Systems must be resilient against adversarial attacks, data poisoning, and prompt injection attempts.
