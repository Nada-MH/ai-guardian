# SDAIA AI Ethics Principles
**Source Identifier**: `SDAIA_AI_ETHICS`  
**Publishing Body**: Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority (SDAIA)  
**Version**: 1.0 (2023)  
**Category**: Core AI Governance  

---

## Principle 1: Fairness and Non-Discrimination

### Clause 1.1: Equitable AI Outcomes
AI systems must be designed and operated to produce equitable outcomes across all demographic groups, with active monitoring and mitigation of algorithmic bias throughout the AI lifecycle.

### Clause 1.2: Prohibited Discriminatory Practices
AI-driven decision-making processes must not discriminate based on nationality, tribal affiliation, religion, gender, disability, or socioeconomic status. Financial AI systems must apply uniform decisioning criteria, except where differentiation is explicitly mandated by law.

---

## Principle 2: Transparency and Explainability

### Clause 2.1: Disclosure of Automated Decisions
Organizations deploying AI systems that impact individual rights, financial access, or public services must disclose the use of automated processing and provide accessible explanations of how decisions were reached.

### Clause 2.2: Algorithmic Auditability
AI systems must maintain audit trails sufficient to enable independent third-party review and reproducibility of automated decisions.

---

## Principle 3: Safety, Security, and Reliability

### Clause 3.1: Continuous Risk Assessment
AI systems must undergo continuous risk assessment throughout their operational lifecycle, with mechanisms for detecting and responding to emerging threats, performance degradation, and unintended behaviors.

### Clause 3.2: Resilience Against Adversarial Attacks
AI systems operating in critical domains must implement protections against adversarial manipulation, including input validation, model robustness testing, and integrity verification of data pipelines.

---

## Principle 4: Privacy and Data Protection

### Clause 4.1: Compliance with Saudi PDPL
All AI systems processing personal data must comply with the Saudi Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), including lawful basis for processing, data minimization, purpose limitation, and data subject rights.

### Clause 4.2: Privacy by Design
AI systems must incorporate privacy-by-design principles, embedding data protection safeguards into the architecture and operational processes from inception rather than as an afterthought.

---

## Principle 5: Human Oversight and Control

### Clause 5.1: Meaningful Human Control
High-risk AI systems must maintain meaningful human oversight, with clear mechanisms for human operators to understand, intervene in, and override automated decisions when necessary.

### Clause 5.2: Escalation Mechanisms
AI systems must incorporate escalation procedures ensuring that decisions with significant financial, legal, or personal impact can be referred to qualified human decision-makers.

---

## Principle 6: Accountability and Responsibility

### Clause 6.1: Clear Accountability Assignment
Organizations must designate individuals or governance bodies accountable for the design, deployment, and outcomes of AI systems, with clearly documented roles and responsibilities.

### Clause 6.2: Remediation and Redress
Organizations must establish mechanisms for affected parties to seek review, correction, or redress of AI-driven decisions, including accessible complaint channels and timely resolution processes.

---

## Principle 7: Sustainability and Social Benefit

### Clause 7.1: Societal Impact Assessment
AI deployments must consider broader societal impacts, including effects on employment, social equity, and environmental sustainability, with preference given to applications that advance social benefit.
