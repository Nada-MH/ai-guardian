# Model Risk Management (MRM) Principles for Banking Institutions
**Source Identifier**: `MRM_SR_11_7`  
**Publishing Body**: Federal Reserve Board / OCC / SAMA Adaptation  
**Version**: SR 11-7  
**Category**: Finance Regulatory  

---

## Section 1: Model Development, Implementation, and Use

### Clause 1.1: Model Conceptual Soundness
Bank AI/ML models must demonstrate rigorous conceptual soundness, supported by empirical evidence, mathematical theory, and financial domain logic. The model architecture, choice of algorithms, and variable selections must be thoroughly documented.

### Clause 1.2: Training and Testing Data Quality
Data used to build and calibrate financial models must be representative of historical business conditions, stress environments, and target customer populations. Data cleaning, imputation methods, and missing value logic must be rigorously audited.

---

## Section 2: Model Validation & Independent Review

### Clause 2.1: Independent Model Validation Function
Model validation must be performed by staff who are independent of model development and operation, possessing appropriate technical competence and authority to challenge model assumptions and results.

### Clause 2.2: Benchmarking and Sensitivity Analysis
Validation must include sensitivity testing across extreme financial scenarios and benchmarking model outcomes against alternative statistical algorithms or baseline heuristic models.

---

## Section 3: Governance, Policies, and Inventory

### Clause 3.1: Comprehensive Model Inventory
Financial institutions must maintain an active, centralized inventory listing all models in development, validation, or production, documenting model ownership, vendor components, risk classification, and re-validation schedules.

### Clause 3.2: Vendor and Third-Party Model Governance
Use of third-party vendor AI models does not exempt financial institutions from Model Risk Management obligations. Institutions must require vendors to provide sufficient technical documentation, validation certificates, and explainability mechanisms.
